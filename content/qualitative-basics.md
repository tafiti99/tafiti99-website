# Qualitative Research: It's Not Just "Talking to People"

**Author:** Sadick A. Tunda  
**Category:** Methodology  
**Read Time:** 5 Minutes

---

There is a misconception that Qualitative research is "easier" than Quantitative research because there are no calculations. This is false. Qualitative analysis requires deep critical thinking to turn words into patterns (Themes).

If your study involves "In-Depth Interviews" (IDIs) or "Focus Group Discussions" (FGDs), here is what you need to know.

## 1. When to use Qualitative?
Use it when you want to answer **"Why?"** or **"How?"**
* *Quantitative:* "40% of women don't use contraceptives." (The Number)
* *Qualitative:* "Women fear contraceptives because of myths about infertility." (The Reason)

## 2. Sample Size: The "Saturation" Point
In quantitative studies, we calculate sample size (e.g., N=384). In qualitative, we use **Saturation**.
* You interview people until you stop hearing new information.
* Usually, this happens after **10-15 interviews**.
* **Do not** write "I will interview 50 people." That is a waste of time. Write: *"Participants will be recruited until data saturation is reached."*

## 3. Analysis: From Codes to Themes
You cannot just summarize what people said. You must code the data.
1.  **Transcript:** Type out every word from the recording.
2.  **Coding:** Highlight key phrases (e.g., "transport cost," "fear of rude nurses").
3.  **Themes:** Group codes together.
    * *Example:* "Transport cost" + "Service fee" = **Theme: Financial Barriers**.

## 4. Trustworthiness (Not Validity)
We don't use "P-values" here. We use **Trustworthiness**.
* **Credibility:** Did you spend enough time with participants?
* **Transferability:** Can these findings apply to other similar settings?
* **Confirmability:** Can you show your raw data (transcripts) if asked?
