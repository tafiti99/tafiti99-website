# How to Survive Your Thesis Defense: Secrets from the Panelists

**Author:** Laurent Chandi  
**Category:** Defense Preparation  
**Read Time:** 6 Minutes

---

The thesis defense is not an interrogation; it is a performance. You have spent months writing your report, but your grade often depends on the 20 minutes you spend presenting it.

Having watched countless defenses at MUHAS, I have noticed that students don't fail because they did bad research; they fail because they cannot *explain* their research. Here is how to own the room.

## 1. The Structure of a Winning Presentation
You likely have 10-15 minutes. Do not waste time reading every bullet point. Structure your slides like this:
* **Slide 1-2: Background & Problem.** (Why should we care? What is the gap?)
* **Slide 3: Objectives.** (Read these verbatim. Do not paraphrase.)
* **Slide 4-5: Methodology.** (Briefly: Design, Site, Sample Size, Analysis).
* **Slide 6-10: Results.** (The core. Use Graphs/Tables. **Do not put paragraphs here.**)
* **Slide 11: Discussion/Conclusion.** (What does it mean?)
* **Slide 12: Recommendations.** (Practical steps for the Ministry or Hospital).

## 2. The "So What?" Question
This is the question that kills most presentations. A panelist will ask:
> *"Okay, you found that 40% of students are anxious. So what?"*

**The Wrong Answer:** "So... it is a problem."  
**The Right Answer:** "This high prevalence suggests that current school-based counseling interventions are insufficient. It implies we need a policy shift towards peer-support groups..."

Always connect your number to a real-world implication.

## 3. How to Handle "I Don't Know"
You will get a question you cannot answer. Do not panic, and do not lie.
* **Do NOT say:** "I forgot to check that."
* **DO say:** "That is an excellent point. Our study focused on [X], so we did not collect data on [Y], but it would be a valuable variable for future research."

This turns a weakness ("I didn't do it") into a scientific limitation ("It was out of scope").

## 4. Know Your Weaknesses Before They Do
Every study has a flaw. Maybe your sample size was small (N=150), or you used a non-probability sampling method.
* **The trick:** Admit it before they attack it.
* *Say:* "One limitation of this study was the cross-sectional design, which limits our ability to establish causality..."
* If you say it first, you look self-aware and critical. If they point it out first, you look negligent.

## 5. Visuals Matter
Your slides are for the audience, not for you.
* **Font Size:** Minimum 24pt.
* **Graphs:** Copying a table from SPSS is often ugly. Re-draw it in Excel or PowerPoint.
* **Text:** 5 bullet points max per slide.

> **Final Advice:** Confidence comes from preparation. If you know your data inside out, you have nothing to fear.
