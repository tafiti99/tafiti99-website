# Literature Review: Stop Reading, Start Writing

**Author:** Laurent Chandi  
**Category:** Academic Writing  
**Read Time:** 6 Minutes

---

The Literature Review is where most students get stuck. You download 50 PDFs, read them all, and then stare at a blank screen. This is the wrong approach. You are not reading for fun; you are reading for **evidence**.

Here is how to write a solid literature review without getting a headache.

## 1. The "Funnel" Structure
Structure your review like a funnel: **Global -> Regional -> Local.**
1.  **Global:** What is the situation worldwide? (e.g., "WHO reports that Diabetes affects 10% of the global population...")
2.  **Regional:** What is happening in Sub-Saharan Africa? (e.g., "In Kenya and Uganda, prevalence is rising due to urbanization...")
3.  **Local:** What is the data in Tanzania? (e.g., "However, data in Dar es Salaam is limited...")

## 2. Don't Summarize, Synthesize
* **Bad (The Laundry List):** "Smith said X. Then Jones said Y. Then Ali said Z."
* **Good (Synthesis):** "While Smith (2019) argues that diet is the main cause, recent studies by Jones (2021) and Ali (2022) suggest that genetics play a larger role."

Group authors who agree with each other. This shows you understand the *debate*, not just the list.

## 3. The "Gap" is Your Best Friend
The entire purpose of the Literature Review is to find a **Gap**.
* "Study A looked at men, but not women."
* "Study B was done in 2010, so the data is old."
* "Study C was qualitative, but we need numbers."

Your study exists to fill this gap. End your review by clearly stating: *"Therefore, this study aims to fill this gap by..."*

## 4. Use Reference Managers (Please!)
Do not type your bibliography manually. You will make mistakes.
* Use **Mendeley** or **Zotero** (both are free).
* They allow you to insert citations in Word with one click.
* They automatically generate your "References" list in the correct MUHAS style (APA/Vancouver).
