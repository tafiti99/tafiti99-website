# From Vague Idea to Approved Concept Note: A 4-Step Formula

**Author:** Sadick A. Tunda  
**Category:** Concept Development  
**Read Time:** 4 Minutes

---

The biggest delay in the research journey isn't data analysis; it's getting your topic approved. Many students spend 3 months bouncing back and forth with their supervisors because their idea is "too broad" or "not researchable."

Here is the **Tafiti99 Formula** to get your Concept Note approved on the first try.

## Step 1: The "Problem" is the Key
Do not start with a topic (e.g., "Malaria in Pregnancy"). Start with a **problem**.
* *Bad:* "I want to study hand hygiene."
* *Good:* "Despite 90% of nurses knowing hand hygiene protocols, infection rates in the neonatal ward remain high. Why is there a gap between knowledge and practice?"

**The Rule:** If you cannot state the problem in one sentence, you don't have a study yet.

## Step 2: The "So What?" Test
Imagine your supervisor asks, "Okay, so you find out that 50% of students are stressed. So what?"
You need a **Justification**.
* Does this research save money?
* Does it save lives?
* Does it change policy?
If the answer is "it's just interesting," find a new topic.

## Step 3: Feasibility Check ( The Budget Trap)
Don't propose a study that requires an MRI scan for 500 people if you have no budget.
* **Population:** Can you easily access these people? (e.g., MUHAS students are easier to reach than rural farmers).
* **Time:** Can this be done in 3 months?
* **Money:** Can you afford the printing and transport costs?

## Step 4: The Title Template
A good research title answers three questions: **What? Who? Where?**
> *Assessment of [Variable X] among [Population Y] in [Location Z].*

*Example:* "Assessment of Adherence to Anti-Retroviral Therapy (What) among Pregnant Women (Who) at Amana Regional Referral Hospital (Where)."
